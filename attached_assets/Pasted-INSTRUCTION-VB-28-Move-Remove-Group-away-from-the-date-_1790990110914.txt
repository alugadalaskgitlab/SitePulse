INSTRUCTION VB-28: Move "Remove Group" away from the date-group toggle on the Vendor Bill line-items table — it sits in a misclick-prone spot

READ THIS FIRST — SCOPE DISCIPLINE
Single, narrow, presentation-only fix. Part A is a short design plan — STOP after Part A and share it before writing implementation code, because this shared component has four call sites and the fix must look/behave identically across all of them. STOP after Part B too. Do not touch `removeItemGroup`'s logic, its `window.confirm` guard, the hire-group/hire-statement block, or anything about how line items are grouped, totaled, or removed — this is purely about where the button sits and how it's visually separated from the expand/collapse control. Do not touch HOME-01, DIESEL-04/05, NAV-01, VB-27, MAT-03/04, VENDOR-01/02/03, PERM-01/02, LABOUR-01, PI-01, or any DPR-13 through DPR-20 work. Do not publish automatically.

BACKGROUND — confirmed by direct code review and a screenshot of the live date-group table on the vendor bill edit screen
`client/src/components/vendor-bills/BillDateGroups.tsx`'s `BillDateGroupRows` renders each date group as a single table row: a full-width ghost `Button` that toggles expand/collapse (showing the date, item count, and subtotal), and — immediately below it, inside the same `<td>`, with no gap or divider — a second ghost `Button` styled `text-destructive` reading "Remove Group" (~line 100-110). `client/src/pages/VendorBills.tsx` uses this same component at three call sites (`scope="edit-all"`, per-category via `scope={\`edit-${cat}\`}`, and per-site inside the else-branch) and additionally has a fourth, inline-duplicated copy of the same pattern for labour sub-groups (~line 3796) that is NOT routed through `BillDateGroupRows` — it builds its own `<tr>`/`<td>` with the same "toggle row, Remove Group directly beneath" layout. All four render "Remove Group" as a full-width, stacked-directly-under-the-toggle control, which is exactly the geometry a user's thumb or mouse passes over right after tapping/clicking the date header to expand it.
The underlying action is not unguarded — `removeItemGroup` in `VendorBills.tsx` (~line 1520) already calls `window.confirm(...)` before removing anything, and already blocks removing hire-group/hire-statement generated items with a toast instead. This instruction is NOT about adding a confirmation — one already exists. It's about the button's placement making a misclick likely enough that a user ends up staring at a confirm dialog they didn't mean to trigger, on a line-items table that can represent real money (subtotals in the screenshot run into thousands of rupees per group).
This is the same class of problem DPR-19 Fix 2 already solved elsewhere in this app (the equipment-row delete button overlapping the expand/collapse toggle in `DprEquipmentCompact.tsx`) — that fix gave the destructive control "real, deliberate spacing... a proper flex-adjacent sibling with a visible gap." Reuse that same established pattern here for consistency across the app rather than inventing a new layout convention.

FIX

PART A — Design/mapping plan (STOP after this, share the plan, wait for go-ahead before building)
a. Confirm your proposed placement for "Remove Group" relative to the toggle button. Options to weigh (propose the one you think fits this row's existing layout best, or a better one, and explain why): (i) move it to the trailing edge of the same row, after the subtotal, separated by a visible gap/divider so it's no longer stacked directly beneath the toggle's tap/click zone; (ii) shrink it to an icon-only trash button with its own padding and a real gap from the toggle, consistent with DPR-19's fix; (iii) only render it when the group is already expanded (so collapsed rows — the ones being rapidly tapped through to scan the bill — never show a destructive control at all). Propose whether that fourth block should now also be routed through `BillDateGroupRows` (removing the duplication) or just have its layout mirrored by hand — your call, explain which is lower-risk given it's a smaller, scoped change.
