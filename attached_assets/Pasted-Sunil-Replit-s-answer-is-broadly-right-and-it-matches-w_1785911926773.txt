Sunil, Replit’s answer is broadly right, and it matches what you and I have now concluded:

The engine is becoming intelligent, but the screens are exposing too much of that intelligence as user input.

That is the real issue.

But I would correct two parts of Replit’s proposed simplification.

Where Replit is right
Work Programme

The user should not have to understand:

Stage numbers;
Front labels;
Qty%;
allocation fractions;
width calculations;
sequence logic;
overlap rules.

Those are internal planning mechanics.

The normal user should mainly tell SitePulse:

where the broad reach is;
which side;
roughly when it should be taken up;
whether it is available or blocked.

SitePulse should do the rest.

DPR

The Site Engineer should not have to navigate through a technical planning structure.

The ordinary DPR flow should be:

Select or confirm today’s activity.
Enter actual chainage.
Confirm side.
Enter quantity.
Add photo where required.
Submit or save draft.

Programme linkage, arrangement context, units and expected activities should normally be prefilled.

So the overall philosophy Replit suggested is correct:

The app proposes; the user confirms or corrects.

Where I disagree with Replit
1. Side must not be hidden under “Adjust details”

Replit suggested reducing the stretch dialog to:

From Km;
To Km;
optional label;

and moving Side into advanced details.

For road work, that is not suitable.

You have repeatedly explained the real execution unit:

Reach + Chainage + Side

LHS and RHS are physically separate works. They affect:

planned quantity;
programme bars;
DPR linking;
executed balance;
chainage reports;
overlap detection.

Therefore, Side must stay in the simple default planning flow.

The minimum Work Programme input should be:

Chainage From
Chainage To
Side
Execution Order

Label can be automatically generated and optionally edited.

2. DPR cannot be only “quantity + photo”

That may be too little.

The minimum reliable DPR progress line should still contain:

work item;
actual chainage From–To;
side;
quantity;
photo where required.

The app may prefill item, side and planned reach, but chainage is necessary for the reporting you want:

which stretch was executed;
which portion remains;
gaps;
overlaps;
item-wise chainage register;
draft RA support.

So the minimal DPR should be:

Activity + Chainage + Side + Quantity

Photo is supporting evidence, not a replacement for location.

The simplified Work Programme I recommend
Step 1 — Choose a planning pattern

Offer practical starting buttons:

One continuous front

For a simple project worked in chainage order.

LHS first, then RHS

A common road-execution pattern.

LHS and RHS in parallel

Where there are two work fronts.

Define my own reaches

For irregular availability and blocked portions.

These presets should fill the initial table. They should not permanently dictate the programme.

Step 2 — Confirm major Reach + Side rows

The default table should show only:

Reach	From	To	Side	Execution
Reach A	0.000	1.900	LHS	First
Reach B	0.000	1.900	RHS	Parallel
Reach C	1.900	3.800	LHS	Next

Use plain wording:

First
Next
Later
Parallel with Reach A

Do not show S1, S2, Stage, or Front in the normal flow.

Internally, SitePulse may still store Stage and Front.

Step 3 — SitePulse applies the BOQ items

SitePulse identifies:

Earthwork;
GSB;
WMM;
Prime;
Tack;
DBM;
BC;
structures and other applicable items.

It proposes which items apply to every Reach + Side.

The user only corrects exceptional cases:

item already completed;
item not applicable;
another contractor’s scope;
design pending.
Step 4 — SitePulse allocates BOQ quantities

All programme quantities remain in the BOQ UOM.

SitePulse should automatically use:

side-aware length allocation for normal linear items;
approximate reach/side allocation for earthwork;
BOQ-UOM proportional allocation for MT items;
existing location quantities when available;
manual override only when needed.

Then show a simple review:

GSB — fully programmed
WMM — fully programmed
Earthwork — 92% programmed, 8% not allocated
DBM — fully programmed

The complex arithmetic can remain behind View calculation.

Step 5 — Generate the programme

One primary action:

Generate Programme

The safety preview can run automatically and appear before final confirmation only when something needs attention.

For an ordinary valid plan, it should feel like one simple action.

The simplified Gantt

The normal row should show:

Reach A · Ch. 0+000–1+900 · LHS
GSB · 1,250 CUM · 05–10 Aug

Only show one meaningful exception where necessary:

Blocked
Quantity review
Arrangement required
Delayed

Do not show:

S1/S2/S3;
Front A/Front B;
multiple overlap pills;
repeated outsourced quantities;
repeated over/under values;
internal calculation labels.

Stage/front details may appear in the edit panel or tooltip.

The simplified DPR

When the Site Engineer opens today’s DPR, SitePulse should show the activities that are:

planned today;
active;
recently worked;
part of the same selected site/reach.

Example:

Today’s likely activities
GSB — Reach A — LHS
Earthwork — Reach C — RHS
WMM — Reach B — LHS

The engineer taps one and gets:

Item: prefilled
Planned Reach: prefilled
Side: prefilled
From Chainage
To Chainage
Quantity
Photo
Save

Everything else goes under Add details:

equipment;
labour;
weather;
fuel;
remarks;
testing;
agency;
problems.

But those extra sections can also be intelligently prefilled from:

yesterday’s DPR;
planned execution arrangement;
equipment assigned to site;
standard crews.
“Same as yesterday” is a very good idea

This may be one of the most useful DPR improvements.

It should copy:

active work items;
reach;
side;
equipment;
labour categories;
agency;
standard remarks structure.

It should clear:

actual chainages;
quantities;
readings;
photos;

That could reduce daily DPR effort substantially.

Clear responsibility of each module
Work Programme

What do we plan to execute, where and when?

DPR / Site Report

What exactly did we execute today?

BOM & Demand

What materials, equipment, labour and fuel are required for the programme?

Procurement

What should be ordered or arranged after considering stock, orders and outsourcing?

Stores

What is received, issued, returned and currently available?

Progress & Measurements

What has been executed by item, reach, side and chainage, and what remains?

Draft RA Support

What reported quantities may be considered for measurement and billing review?

That should be visible to users in the navigation descriptions and empty-state guidance.

Are we going in the wrong direction?

No, but we are at the point where we must change how the product is presented.

The intelligence already developed is valuable:

SDB/SNL mapping;
demand calculation;
execution arrangements;
side-aware programme;
chainage validation;
safe regeneration;
programme linkage.

We should not remove it.

But we should now hide most of it below the surface.

The right principle is:

Complex engine, simple screen.
