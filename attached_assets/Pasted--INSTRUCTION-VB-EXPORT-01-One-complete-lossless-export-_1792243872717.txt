# INSTRUCTION — VB-EXPORT-01

**One complete, lossless export of every vendor bill — assembled once, so the Tally
export can reuse the same assembly rather than rebuild it.**

Baseline: the published commit carrying VB-FIND-01 (or whichever is current — state it
in your report).

**Read-only. No calculation changes, no migrations, no new columns, no data changes.**
Every figure on every bill identical before and after.

---

## Why this is built this way

Two things need the same information:

1. **Now** — the owner and his adviser need to read every bill outside the app, to
   reconcile against Tally and decide what needs correcting.
2. **Next** — the Tally export has to assemble exactly the same facts and render them
   as vouchers.

So **the assembly is written once and the rendering is separate.** Do not write an
export that formats as it reads; a later Tally renderer must be able to call the same
assembly and get the same answer. If these diverge, the app will one day report one
set of figures and post another.

---

## Standing rules

- No production data changes, migrations, automatic repairs or publishing without our
  review.
- No sequence resets. Do not call or re-expose `resetAllSequences`.
- **Preserve all existing users, credentials, site access, permissions and
  administrator privileges. Do not grant, revoke or reset anything, and do not add,
  rename or remove any permission key.**
- **Frozen snapshots are read, never re-derived.** Every amount in the export is the
  amount the bill already holds. Nothing is recalculated on the way out.
- Report against the benchmark in force at the baseline commit.

---

## Part A — The assembly, written once

**Required.** A function in `shared/` — a name such as `buildVendorBillExport` — that
takes the bills already loaded and authorised, and returns, per bill, the complete
picture. No database access inside it, no formatting, no rounding beyond what the
stored values carry.

Per bill it returns:

**Identity** — bill id, bill number, bill date, period from, period to, bill type,
category, site, supplied-to party.

**Vendor** — the recorded vendor name exactly as typed, the linked vendor master id
and name where one exists, and a plain flag for linked or unlinked. **Do not
normalise or correct the recorded name.**

**Status** — current status, and for each of draft, verified, approved and paid: who
and when, as stored, with "not recorded" where a value is absent. Plus the voided
flag, void reason, who voided and when.

**Money, each component separately and labelled** — gross, and then **each adjustment
as its own field with its own reason**: breakdown deduction, HSD recovery, other
debit, **advance adjustment**, other credit. Then net, TDS as recorded, amount paid,
balance.

> **The advance adjustment must never be merged into the paid figure, and must be
> labelled so that a reader cannot mistake it for a payment.** It is a deduction that
> reduces the net. This is the single most important distinction in the whole export:
> a reader who confuses the two will double-count the vendor's money.

**Payments** — every recorded payment against the bill, each with its date, amount,
mode and whatever account or reference is stored. If no per-payment record exists and
only a total is held, say so explicitly in the row rather than inventing payments.

**Line items** — description, quantity, unit, rate, amount, and any BOQ reference.

**Hire** — for a bill carrying hire statements: each statement's id, period, status,
and the frozen snapshot's own totals **as stored**. Do not recompute a hire figure.

**Provenance** — created by, created at, and a plain flag for whether a frozen
calculation snapshot exists.

**Constraints.**

- Voided bills are **included and clearly marked**, never silently dropped. A reader
  reconciling against Tally must be able to see what was voided.
- Do not change `billReviewAmounts`, `summarizeBillReview`, `billPaymentPosition`,
  `getBillFinancialTotals`, `filterFinancialBills`, `vendorStatementBills` or
  `projectVendorPayables`. The assembly **calls** them; it does not reimplement them.
- Where a value is absent, the export says so. **No field is ever filled with a
  guess, a substitute date, or a zero standing in for "unknown".**

---

## Part B — Two renderings, one assembly

**Required.**

1. `GET /api/vendor-bills/export?format=json` — the assembly's output as JSON,
   complete and nested. This is the one I will read.
2. `GET /api/vendor-bills/export?format=xlsx` — a workbook for the owner to look at:
   one row per bill with the money columns in the order above, a second sheet of line
   items keyed to the bill number, and a third of payments. Follow the existing
   workbook conventions in `server/vendorFinancialWorkbook.ts`; reuse its helpers
   rather than writing new ones.
3. Optional filters, all defaulting to **everything**: vendor, bill type, status,
   date range, site. The unfiltered call must return every bill in the caller's
   permitted scope, voided included.
4. A header block on both: when it was generated, by whom, how many bills, and the
   filters in force (or "none").

**Constraints.**

- **Read-only. It creates, updates and deletes nothing**, and does not touch any
  cached or derived table.
- Both formats come from the same assembly call. **Do not special-case a figure for
  one format.**

---

## Part C — Who may run it

**Required.** The export requires **View Vendor Financials**, and is restricted to
Administrator or Owner, and is site-scoped exactly as the Vendor Bills list is. A
caller who cannot see a site's bills does not receive them, and the header states the
scope that was applied.

**Constraints.** Do not widen any existing permission. Do not add a permission key.
If the existing financial guard already covers this shape, put the route behind it
rather than writing a new check.

---

## Part D — Prove it is complete

This is the part that matters most. An export that quietly omits bills is worse than
no export, because every conclusion drawn from it will be wrong and look right.

**Required, reported as numbers:**

1. The count of bills in the database, the count in the unfiltered export, and
   confirmation they are equal. If they differ, **name every missing bill and why**.
2. The sum of gross, net, paid and balance across the export, set beside the same four
   figures from the payables page, unfiltered. **They must agree.**
3. The sum of advance adjustments across the export, stated on its own — and
   confirmation that this figure is **not** included in the paid total.
4. The count of voided bills, and confirmation they are present and marked.
5. The count of unlinked bills, and confirmation their recorded names are unchanged.
6. One bill reproduced in full in the report — `HLC/VB/2026/0054` — every field, so
   the shape can be checked by eye.
