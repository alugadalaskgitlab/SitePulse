Instruction 022 — Execution Responsibility, Progress Reforecasting and Daily Diesel Architecture

Purpose

Design a commercially strong project-controls feature that keeps SitePulse procurement, equipment, labour and diesel planning realistic when:

- work is executed in-house, outsourced, partly outsourced or client-supplied;
- actual progress falls behind the original programme;
- overdue work cannot practically be executed all at once;
- diesel is normally arranged daily from Tomorrow’s Plan rather than purchased only through bulk PI;
- PM/Admin needs a realistic current forecast without losing the original baseline.

THIS IS A DESIGN, DISCOVERY AND ARCHITECTURE TASK FIRST.

Do not begin broad implementation until the existing system has been inspected and the architecture has been reviewed and approved.

Primary objectives

1. Preserve the original approved baseline programme permanently.
2. Track actual progress separately from the baseline.
3. Support a current operational forecast/revised programme without overwriting the baseline.
4. Separate overdue backlog from realistically executable demand.
5. Calculate materials, equipment, labour and diesel only for the party responsible for them.
6. Support Daily Diesel Requirement from Tomorrow’s Plan.
7. Retain Bulk Diesel PI for projects that purchase and store diesel in bulk.
8. Prevent duplicate calculations or parallel features where similar logic already exists.

DO NOT TOUCH OR IMPLEMENT YET

- existing Work Programme dates or quantities
- current Work Demand calculation
- UOM conversion logic
- material mapping
- procurement-status logic
- PI/IRN/GRN workflows
- Stores stock
- Plant production
- DPR entry
- Tomorrow’s Plan
- equipment usage
- diesel calculations
- subcontract billing
- profitability calculations
- user permissions

Do not create migrations, schema changes, pages or transactional records in this discovery stage.

Required calculation principle:

Only components assigned to HLC should generate HLC:

- material demand
- equipment demand
- labour demand
- diesel demand
- internal production cost

Outsourced components should instead generate:

- subcontract quantity
- unit rate
- estimated subcontract value
- measurement/progress obligation
- vendor liability

Do not double-count HLC materials, equipment or diesel where the agency’s composite rate includes them.

Phase 4 — Design baseline, actual and forecast programme structure

The system must maintain three separate concepts.

A. Baseline Programme

- original approved plan
- permanently preserved
- never overwritten
- used for delay and performance comparison

B. Actual Progress

- derived from submitted/approved DPR quantities
- linked to BOQ item, site, reach and dates
- cumulative actual quantity
- actual production rate
- actual start and latest working date

C. Current Forecast / Working Programme

- realistic remaining execution plan
- may be revised when work slips
- does not alter the baseline
- must carry:
  - version number
  - revision date
  - reason
  - prepared by
  - approved by/status
  - effective date

Required comparison:

- baseline start/finish
- actual progress
- current forecast finish
- delay against baseline
- current production rate
- required recovery rate
- balance quantity

Do not call an edited baseline a revised baseline.

Every operational change should create a new forecast version or controlled revision.

Phase 5 — Design backlog versus executable demand

The system must not move all overdue quantity into today or the selected horizon.

For every activity/material, distinguish:

1. Baseline Overdue Backlog

Quantity that should have been completed under the baseline but remains incomplete.

2. Executable Demand in Selected Horizon

Quantity realistically planned under the current approved forecast or short-term programme.

3. Future Balance

Remaining quantity planned after the selected horizon.

Example:

- overdue backlog: 3,000 CUM
- planned during next 7 days: 700 CUM
- future balance after horizon: 2,300 CUM

Procurement, equipment, labour and diesel action should normally be based on the executable 700 CUM, not the entire 3,000 CUM.

The backlog must remain visible as a management warning.

If no approved forecast exists:

- do not assume all backlog is due immediately;
- use the latest approved Tomorrow’s Plan or short-term programme for immediate requirements;
- display:
  “Reforecast required — near-term demand is based on the latest approved short-term plan.”

Phase 6 — Design programme-drift detection

Design a warning system that identifies when actual progress materially differs from the current programme.

Possible triggers:

- actual quantity behind planned quantity beyond tolerance
- activity continuing after forecast finish date
- no progress for a configurable number of days
- actual average output materially below planned output
- forecast completion moving beyond contractual milestone

Suggested statuses:

- on_track
- watch
- behind_schedule
- critically_delayed
- blocked
- reforecast_required

The PM/Admin should be offered:

- Continue at current achieved rate
- Enter revised production target
- Record delay/constraint
- Prepare recovery plan
- Create new forecast version

Delay reasons may include:

- rain/weather
- client instruction
- land obstruction
- utility shifting
- drawing/approval delay
- plant breakdown
- equipment breakdown
- material shortage
- fund/payment constraint
- subcontractor delay
- labour shortage
- traffic restriction
- other

Phase 7 — Design recovery planning

For remaining quantity, calculate:

- current achieved daily rate
- rolling recent production rate
- remaining working days
- forecast completion date
- production rate required to meet baseline
- production rate required to meet current milestone

Allow the PM to enter a realistic recovery target.

Do not automatically assume the required recovery rate is achievable.

Where equipment/resources are known, the system may later estimate:

- additional equipment required
- additional shifts
- additional crews
- increased plant production
- increased daily diesel

This calculation must respect Execution & Supply Responsibility.

If the work is outsourced, do not assume HLC can increase production merely by adding HLC equipment.

Phase 8 — Design Daily Diesel Requirement

When Diesel/HSD action is clicked, offer:

1. Prepare Daily Diesel Requirement
2. Raise Bulk Purchase PI

A. Prepare Daily Diesel Requirement

Preferably connect to the existing Tomorrow’s Plan if such logic already exists.

Calculate for the selected day:

- equipment planned
- expected working hours
- consumption per hour
- HLC fuel responsibility
- planned trips or kilometres where applicable
- opening diesel balance
- diesel already arranged/confirmed
- net quantity to arrange

Example:

- excavator: 80 L
- grader: 120 L
- roller: 70 L
- tippers: 350 L
- gross requirement: 620 L
- opening balance: 90 L
- already arranged: 0 L
- net daily requirement: 530 L

The PM/Site Engineer should be able to review and adjust with a reason.

Do not include diesel for:

- fully outsourced composite work where vendor provides fuel;
- vendor transport where vendor bears fuel;
- equipment not planned for that day;
- non-working equipment.

B. Raise Bulk Purchase PI

Retain for:

- project diesel tank
- bulk tanker purchase
- monthly/rate-contract arrangement
- major projects storing diesel
- advance procurement

Do not force ordinary daily diesel through a long-term bulk PI process.

Phase 9 — Define demand hierarchy

Design SitePulse demand at four levels.

Tomorrow:

- driven by Tomorrow’s Plan
- daily diesel
- immediate material
- equipment
- labour

Next 7/30 days:

- driven by current approved forecast
- procurement
- vendor arrangement
- plant production
- equipment planning

Entire Remaining Programme:

- total balance requirement
- budget
- long-term procurement
- profitability
- capacity planning

Overdue Backlog:

- management warning
- recovery-planning quantity
- not automatically treated as tomorrow’s requirement
